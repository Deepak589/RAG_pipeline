The solar system consists of the Sun and everything bound to it by gravity. Eight planets orbit the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. The four inner planets are rocky terrestrial worlds, while the four outer planets are gas and ice giants.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system, with a mass more than twice that of all the other planets combined. Its Great Red Spot is a giant storm that has raged for centuries. Jupiter has at least 95 known moons, including the four large Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto. Ganymede is the largest moon in the solar system, bigger than the planet Mercury.

Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, made mostly of ice particles with some rocky debris. Saturn's moon Titan has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane, making it one of the most interesting targets for astrobiology research.

Mars, often called the Red Planet, gets its color from iron oxide on its surface. It hosts Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the solar system, standing about 22 kilometers high. Evidence suggests Mars once had liquid water on its surface, and its polar ice caps still contain frozen water today.

The asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter, containing millions of rocky bodies. Beyond Neptune lies the Kuiper Belt, home to dwarf planets like Pluto, and far beyond that the Oort Cloud, the source of long-period comets.
